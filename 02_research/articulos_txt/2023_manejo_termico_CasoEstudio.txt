Handling Editor: Huihe Qiu

Metal hydride (MH), considered a promising hydrogen storage material, has received wide
attention. As an efficient device for hydrogen energy utilization, a proton exchange membrane
fuel cell (PEMFC) generates power and releases waste heat. MH tank can supply hydrogen for
PEMFC, and the waste heat produced by PEMFC can be applied to heat the MH bed for dehy­
driding reaction. Thermal management between MH tank and PEMFC has been studied in this
work. A lumped parameter model for the PEMFC-MH system has been developed and validated.
The performance of the PEMFC-MH system has been predicted, the results show that it is feasible
to use waste heat produced by PEMFC for heating the MH bed. The effect of the valve’s opening
degree on system performance has been investigated. The performance of the PEMFC-MH system
with various output powers has been studied. The hydrogen supply rate from the MH bed to
PEMFC can be adjusted based on different power requirements. The valve’s opening degree can
be considered an effective control measure in thermal management to improve the energy effi­
ciency of the PEMFC-MH system.

1. Introduction
The shipping industry plays an important role in the national economy. However, traditional diesel-powered ships consume a lot of
fossil energy with a large amount of CO2 emitted. Various governments and enterprises actively seek solutions to reduce CO2 emissions
from shipping in the face of global climate change [1]. The hydrogen-powered ships have received wide attention [2,3]. Considering
the small power requirement and short route for inland vessels, the inland river may be one of the main application scenarios of
hydrogen-powered ships [4]. Of course, effective hydrogen storage methods for the shipping industry are underway, which is essential
to wide applications of hydrogen energy.
As a kind of effective hydrogen storage material, metal hydride (MH) has been successfully applied to the submarine [5] and shows

the possibility of its application in the light vehicle [6] and the waterway transportation field [2]. Considering the relatively low
operating pressure of hydrogen storage systems based on MHs, it may cause the refueling cost and the hydrogen price to decrease [7],
which is good for promoting hydrogen applications. As a kind of common MH, LaNi5 hydrides have some advantages, such as low
operating temperature and platform pressure, and have received extensive research [8–12]. Compared with some high-temperature
MHs, such as Mg-based hydride [13], its gravimetric hydrogen density is relatively small. However, compared with the gravimetric
energy density of the Li-ion battery systems in the ships (about 111 Wh/kg [14]), MHs present better energy storage performance when
the stored hydrogen is converted into electric energy by PEMFC.
In general, the hydriding/dehydriding reaction is accompanied by obvious exothermic/endothermic phenomenon, which leads to
significant thermal effect problems and affects system performance. Various heat exchangers, such as metal foams [8,9] and cooling
tubes [11], have been applied in MH reactors to mitigate the thermal effects of the chemical reaction processes. Metal foam with
relatively large thermal conductivity can improve the heat transfer performance of MH beds by increasing its effective thermal
conductivity [8]. Phase change material (PCM) jacket can be used as a passive heat exchanger, which absorbs heat during hydriding
and releases it during dehydriding in the MH system [15–17]. Considering the relatively low thermal conductivity of PCM, it can work
with metal foam [15] or expanded graphite [16] and form composited PCM with higher thermal conductivity, which is proven to be
feasible to improve the hydriding rate of MH systems. MH reactor with PCM has been applied to supply hydrogen for fuel cell ap­
plications [16,17]. A cooling tube or heat exchanger can also be used as the bridge to enhance heat transfer between the MH bed and
the PCM jacket, which is good for improving the reaction rate and reusing the heat stored in PCM [18].
Thermal management between MH and other equipment has received wide attention. The feasibility of cooling the battery pack
with the MH during dehydriding has been investigated by experiments for a hybrid electric vehicle [19]. In recent years, many re­
searchers have been trying to reuse waste heat as the by-product of PEMFC, which makes it possible to supply heat for MH beds
[20–22]. Proton exchange membrane fuel cells can be considered an efficient device utilization of hydrogen energy, where there are
complicated electrochemical and thermal processes [23,24]. Based on the experimental data in Ref. [25], the thermal management of
the PEMFC stack for a vehicle has been investigated [26]. The heat produced by the PEMFC stack can be directly transferred to MH
tank by using electric fan [22], which can be considered a simple and feasible way. However, the hot air from the fan is used to heat the
outer wall of the MH tank. Normally, directly heating the MH bed rather than the wall of the tank will show better results.
Some special devices based on heat exchanger material have been designed and developed as the bridge for strengthening the heat
transfer between the PEMFC and the MH bed. As an efficient heat exchanger, the heat pipe can be used as the thermal bridge between
them, which can effectively improve the hydrogen supply rate of the hydrogen storage tank [27]. Better performance can be obtained
when the heat pipe is covered by fins [28]. As for the PEMFC with relatively large output power, the liquid cooling mode is more
popular than the air cooling mode. It will consume electric energy generated by the PEMFC stack when the cooling system works. Some
waste heat released by PEMFC is transferred to the MH bed, then the energy consumption of the auxiliary cooling sub-system of PEMFC
decreases, which leads to an increase in the system generation efficiency. As for a hydrogen-powered vehicle where the waste heat
released by PEMFC is transferred to a MH tank through a fan and radiator, research results show that this integration equipment can
save 10% fuel [29]. In the thermal management module of the PEMFC-MH system, the hydrogen storage tank and the heat exchanger
can be connected in serial [30] or parallel [21,29] in the circulating water system. Generally, it is good for controlling the dehydriding
rate by adjusting the valve opening for the parallel mode. The PEMFC-MH system was applied to the first Italian hydrogen propulsion
ship [2], and its corresponding control method for satisfying various load demands plays an important role in the practical application
process [31].
In our previous work [32], the dehydriding performance of MH tank has been studied based on the validated distributed numerical
model, and the hydrogen fuel supply rate of this MH reactor for applications can be fitted as an equation about the pressure at the outlet
and the temperature of heating water. Meanwhile, some lumped parameter models of MH reactors have been validated [8,9] and
applied for hydrogen storage and purification [33]. This work has developed a lumped parameter model for the MH hydrogen storage
tank coupled with PEMFC. The feasibilities of directly supplying hydrogen from the MH tank to PEMFC and using waste heat produced
by PEMFC for heating MH bed during dehydriding have been evaluated, and the thermal management of this PEMFC-MH system has
been studied under different output power conditions.
2. Mathematical model
The basic governing equations for the lumped parameter model of the PEMFC-MH system have been introduced in this section. This
lumped parameter model mainly consists of the PEMFC stack, the MH hydrogen storage tank and the circulating water sub-system used
for the thermal management between the PEMFC stack and the MH tank. The MH tank can be considered a hydrogen source to supply
high-purity fuel for the PEMFC system. The PEMFC stack generates electricity by producing much heat by consuming the charged
hydrogen. The thermal management sub-system is designed to transfer adequate waste heat from the PEMFC to the MH tank during
dehydriding and transfer the rest of heat to the environment by the heat exchanger to maintain that the PEMFC system works at the
proper temperature. This method is not only good for MH tank to supply enough heat in the dehydriding process but also good for
PEMFC system to decrease the energy consumption of the cooling device and improve the system efficiency.

2.3. Thermal management between PEMFC and metal hydride tank
The thermal management sub-system plays an important role in maintaining the normal operation of the PEMFC-MH system. As for
the PEMFC-MH system in the present work, a portion of the heat produced by the PEMFC stack can be used to heat the MH bed for
quick hydrogen release. It provides a feasible way for the waste heat utilization of PEMFC. The heat exchanger transfers the rest waste
heat from the circulating water to the environment.
The diagram of the PEMFC-MH system is shown in Fig. 1. The circulating water in the tube is used as the heat exchange medium
among the PEMFC stack, the MH bed and the heat exchanger. The circulating water with a relatively high temperature flows out from
the PEMFC stack, and flows to the MH tank and the heat exchanger through a three-way valve. Then, the circulating water with a
relatively low temperature from these two devices flows into the PEMFC stack through another three-way valve. The mass flow rate of
circulating water flowing through the PEMFC stack ṁW FC [kg/s] can be controlled by the pump.
The energy balance equation of the circulating water between the inlet and the outlet of the PEMFC stack section can be expressed
as,

3. Model parameters and validation
3.1. Model parameters
A lumped parameter model of the PEMFC stack is applied to the PEMFC-MH system. As a power generation device, the PEMFC
produces electricity and releases waste heat as a by-product by consuming hydrogen. In order to meet the temperature limitation of
proton exchange membranes, the operation temperature of the PEMFC stack is almost below 90 ◦ C, which is dependent on the thermal
management strategy of the PEMFC system. In this work, the operation temperature is set to be constant. The PEMFC stack consists of
45 single fuel cells. The active area of the single fuel cell is 50 cm2 and its thickness of the membrane is 0.00508 cm, more information
about the single cell can be found in Refs. [25,26].
As shown in Fig. 1, the hydrogen storage tank is equipped with a straight tube heat exchanger for heating the MH bed during
dehydriding. The outer diameter of the tank d1 is 0.13 m and its inner diameter d2 is 0.11 m. The total length of the MH tank is 0.6 m,
and the length of the cooling tube in the MH bed L is 0.58 m. The outer diameter of the cooling tube d3 is 0.012 m, and the effect of the
steel wall of the cooling tube on system performance is ignored in this lumped parameter model. The heat transfer area AW_MH between
the MH bed and the circulating water has been calculated and set as about 0.02187 m2. The metal hydride LaNi5H6 is a kind of common
5

Case Studies in Thermal Engineering 43 (2023) 102812

hydrogen storage material which can release hydrogen at room temperature. It also shows good performance of hydriding/dehy­
driding reaction kinetics. Of course, some other MHs with a relatively low operation temperature, such as TiFe hydrides, can be applied
to the PEMFC-MH system in the future. The hydrogen storage tank is filled with this material, and the volume of the MH bed is about
70% of the total volume of this tank [21]. The change in the volume of the MH bed during dehydriding is neglected, and the porosity of
the MH bed is assumed to be constant (0.5 [-]). The solid material is in the saturated state at the initial stage, and its density is equal to
7259 kg/m3 [10,15]. There is about 12.95 kg of MH in the tank, which can be used to store about 177.4 g of H2. The initial temperature
and the initial pressure are set as 323 K and 4.576 bar, respectively. More information on the parameters used in the simulation of the
PEMFC-MH system is presented in Table 1.
3.2. Model validation
Based on the mathematical equations and the parameters above, the lumped parameter model of PEMFC can be developed on the
software platform Matlab/Simulink. The flowchart of calculation for the PEMFC-MH system has been presented in Fig. 2. Based on the
power requirement of load, the hydrogen supply rate from the MH tank to PEMFC is determined. Then, PEMFC can generate power for
a load with much heat released. By setting the opening degree of the valve in the thermal management of the PEMFC-MH system, the
waste heat can be reused for a dehydriding reaction. Its simulation results are compared with the data from other published works [25,
26], including the voltage and the thermal power of a single fuel cell at different operation temperatures (323 K and 353 K), as shown
in Fig. 3. In general, the voltage decreases and the thermal power increases with the increment of the current density. The difference
between the present work and others is acceptable, so this PEMFC model can be considered validated.
In addition, various lumped parameter models of hydrogen storage tanks filled with MH have been validated in our previous work
[33]. The MH tank is used to supply hydrogen fuel for PEMFC in order to satisfy the requirement of loads. Some waste heat as the
by-product of PEMFC is applied to heat the MH tank and improve the dehydriding rate through the circulating water system. Basic
energy balance equations are used to describe the thermal management between the MH tank and the PEMFC system, whose cor­
rectness is self-evident. In general, the PEMFC-MH system can be considered validated based on the effectiveness of these sub-system
models. This lumped parameter model can be used as a basic reference to estimate the feasibility of the PEMFC-MH system and find
reasonable operating conditions.
4. Case studies
4.1. Performance prediction of PEMFC-MH system
The performance of the PEMFC-MH system has been predicted in order to evaluate the feasibility of heating the MH bed with the
waste heat of PEMFC.
The hydrogen supply rate of the MH tank ṁH2 is set as 2.25 × 10− 2 g/s (about 15.17 L/min), which is assumed to be equal to the
hydrogen consumption rate of PEMFC. The changes in hydrogen pressure between the MH bed and the PEMFC are not considered in
this work. The hydrogen flowing into the PEMFC is assumed to be consumed completely. The operation temperature of PEMFC TFC and
the opening degree of the three-way valve a are set as 323 K and 0.3, respectively. The temperatures of circulating water at the inlet of
the MH bed and heat exchanger are assumed to be equal to TFC .
The simulation results have been presented in Fig. 4, including the hydrogen pressure in the MH tank, the average temperature of
the MH bed, the hydrogen storage capacity of the MH bed, the heat power of the PEMFC system, the MH bed and the heat exchanger,
the temperatures of circulation water at the inlet and the outlet of MH bed and the temperatures of circulation water at the inlet and the
outlet of heat exchanger. As shown in Fig. 4a, the maximum pressure of hydrogen in the MH tank is about 3.5 bar (0.35 MPa).
Considering the relatively low operating pressure of MHs, the max pressure in the MH reactor is much lower than the normal working
pressure of the compressed hydrogen storage tank (35 MPa/70 MPa). We can use the low-cost metal tank for the MHs.
When the gaseous hydrogen flows into the PEMFC stack from the MH tank at a constant rate, the hydrogen storage capacity of the
MH bed almost decreases linearly, and the hydrogen pressure in the tank decreases to below 1.1 bar after 2170 s. Under these con­
ditions, it is necessary to equip a device to increase the pressure of hydrogen in order to meet the requirement for the inlet pressure at
the anode.

5. Conclusions
The lumped parameters model for the PEMFC-MH system composed of the PEMFC stack, the MH tank and the thermal management
sub-system has been developed on the Matlab/Simulink software platform based on basic governing equations. Detailed information
on the governing equations of these sub-systems has been presented in this work. The effectiveness of the model for the PEMFC stack
has been validated by the data from literature. The validation of the model of a hydrogen storage tank filled with metal hydrides was
done in our previous work. The model of the PEMFC-MH system with the rated output power of about 1 kW can be considered effective
for studying the system performance under various conditions. A parametric study is applied to the system coupling the MH hydrogen
storage tank and proton exchange membrane fuel cells to improve its energy efficiency. The opening degree of the valve and the flow
rate of hydrogen have been considered in the parametric study. As for the given PEMFC-MH system, when the hydrogen supply rate is
15.17 L/min and the opening degree of the valve is set as 0.5, the MH tank can directly be used to supply the fuel to the fuel cell with
the power of about 1 kW about 2 h. The hydrogen storage system can meet the hydrogen requirement of the fuel cell system when its
output power ranges from 756.3 W to 926 W, where the matching opening degree of the valve has been given. Based on proper thermal
management between the PEMFC stack and the MH tank, the PEMFC-MH system can present a certain degree of autonomy and stably
generate electric energy without additional auxiliary heating equipment during dehydriding.
More work can be done to optimize the relationship between the capacity of the hydrogen storage tank and the output power of
PEMFC. Moreover, how to effectively control the dehydriding rate when the PEMFC is started at low temperatures should be
considered in the future.
