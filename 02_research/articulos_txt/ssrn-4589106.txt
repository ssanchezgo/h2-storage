Abstract：Using metal hydride for hydrogen storage and transportation is a promising
technology due to its advantages of large hydrogen storage capacity, low pressure and

tn

low energy consumption. It is common to combine the metal hydride reactor with phase
change materials to recover the heat generated during the hydrogen absorption and use
it for hydrogen desorption, which can improve the overall energy efficiency. For the
reactor design, the heat transfer rate is a key factor affecting the performance of

rin

hydrogen absorption and desorption. To enhance heat transfer, this paper proposes a
honeycomb fin metal hydride reactor integrated with PCM. Based on simulations,
results show that the achieved hydrogen storage capacity is 1.326 wt.%, the gravimetric

ep

and volumetric storage densities are 0.411% and 14.76 kg of H2 per m3, and the mean
saturated rates are 1.222×10-3 g s-1 and 0.773×10-3 g s-1 for absorption and desorption
process. In addition, the effects of operational parameters such as fin size, thickness of
PCM, hydrogen absorption pressure, and physical properties including solid (liquid)

Pr

thermal conductivity of PCM on the hydrogen absorption and desorption are
investigated. It is found that he small fin size is beneficial to the reaction rate. With the
increase of PCM thickness, the mean saturated rates increase, while the gravimetric and

volumetric storage densities decrease. The impact of operating parameters is also

significant, increasing the PCM thermal conductivity and changing the initial
temperature and hydrogen pressure all can accelerate the reaction rate.

The development of hydrogen energy and the achievement of an effective

hydrogen economy are currently challenged by the available solutions for H2 storage
and transportation. Safety, large capacity, low cost and convenient access are the

requirements for hydrogen storage technology. Metal hydride (MH), as a solid

hydrogen storage technology, is considered as a potential candidate to meet the demand
for hydrogen energy applications due to important advantages, among which the high

pe
er
re
v

volume hydrogen storage capacity, good safety and no explosion risks. However,
during the hydrogen absorption/desorption cycle, there is always a large amount of

reaction heat, which can easily cause the hydrogen absorption/desorption reaction to be
suspended [1]. For example, the hydrogen absorption process is exothermic, which
inevitably leads to an increase in the temperature of the MH bed along with an increase
in the equilibrium pressure. Once the equilibrium pressure is too high to drive the
reaction, the hydrogen absorption process stops, resulting in a rapid reduction in
hydrogenation rate and a loss of H2 storage capacity. Therefore, the improvement and
accurate prediction of heat transfer plays a pivotal role in the design and optimization
of metal hydride reactors to achieve high hydrogen storage efficiency [2].
Many researchers have analyzed the heat and mass transfer characteristics of metal
hydride reactors by establishing mathematical models and experimental studies. Wu et

ot

al. [3] proposed a three-dimensional model of a new magnesium based metal hydride
reactor with spiral coil heat exchanger. Based on the numerical simulation, the results

tn

showed that the spiral coil reactor could effectively improve the hydrogen storage rate.
Bao et al. [4] evaluated the impact of two heat transfer enhancement measures, namely,
using metal fins and making metal hydride powder into compacts. It was concluded that

rin

expanded natural graphite compression technology should be recommended in MHTES
applications, while also removing expensive metal fins. Wang et al. [5] proposed a
radiation microchannel reactor (RMCR) to improve thermal efficiency and developed
a radiation microchannel reactor with a jacket (RMCR-J) to eliminate adverse heat

ep

transfer regions inside the RMCR. Compared with different forms of reactors, radiation
tubes could effectively remove reaction heat and rapidly reduce the temperature of the
MH bed, thereby accelerating the H2 absorption process. Liu et al. [6] proposed a metal

Pr

hydride reactor with variable cross-section annular fins (VCSAF). A thermal coupling
model between the bed and VCSAF was established, and the influence of different fin
structural parameters on the homogeneity of the reactor was studied. The VCSAF
structure had good performance in eliminating non-uniform reaction. With the

storage technology of MH. Bai et al. [7] proposed a metal hydride reactor with coupled

The results showed that compared with the original metal foam reactor and fin reactor,
the time required for the optimized reactor to complete 90% hydrogen charging was

reduced by 6.9% and 38%, respectively. Under the conditions of different materials

with high thermal conductivity, the optimal fin volume ratio of the optimized fin metal
foam reactor is about 0.4.

In this paper, a new type of metal hydride reactor with honeycomb fins and

integrated with phase change material is proposed to achieve uniform reaction and save
time. The honeycomb structure has high structural efficiency and large surface area to

volume ratio, resulting in good heat transfer performance. For this type of reactor,

pe
er
re
v

honeycomb fins are placed inside to increase the heat transfer area, metal hydride is

filled into the space between the fins, and phase change material is surrounded outside.
A three-dimensional reactor model has been built using the COMSOL Multiphysics
V5.6 to predict the reaction process. The influence of the reactor with or without fins
on the hydrogenation process is compared by numerical simulation, and the influence
of the fin size, the PCM thickness, the PCM thermal conductivity and latent heat value,
the porosity of MH and the initial temperature and pressure on the reactor performance
is studied, so as to optimize the reaction parameters of the new structure. The results of
this study can provide reference for using honeycomb fins and PCM as an effective
thermal management method in MH storage systems.

3.1.4.4 Effect of hydrogen absorption pressure

In addition to the influencing factors studied above, hydrogen absorption pressure

is also an important operating parameter of hydrogen storage reaction. Figure 12 shows
the influence of hydrogen pressure (0.6MPa, 0.8MPa, 1.0MPa, 1.2MPa) on the
absorption process. The results indicate that the increase in hydrogen pressure

effectively improves the heat transfer and hydrogen absorption rate. During the

pe
er
re
v

hydrogen absorption process, the temperature first rises rapidly to the highest values

corresponding to different pressures, then maintains for a period of time before rapidly
decreasing, and then decreases at a slower rate. The sharp increase in temperature is
due to the low temperature at the beginning of the reaction, low equilibrium hydrogen
pressure, large reaction driving force (the difference between hydrogen pressure in the
reactor and equilibrium hydrogen pressure), and fast reaction speed; the reaction
fraction sharply increases, and in a short time, a large amount of heat is released from
the reaction, which cannot be transmitted from the bed layer to the phase change
material zone in time, resulting in a sharp increase in bed temperature. After reaching
the highest temperature, the driving force of the reaction begins to decrease, and the
reaction tends to be gentle. The heat generated by the reaction is slowly transferred to

ot

the phase change material, and the heat generated by the reaction is basically in
equilibrium with the heat transferred to the PCM. Therefore, it will be maintained for a
period of time at the highest temperature. Then the temperature rapidly decreases, and

tn

the temperature in the phase change zone rises to the phase change temperature. The
PCM begins to melt, absorbing and storing the heat. Finally, as the reaction approaches
completion and the temperature difference between the reaction zone and the phase

rin

change material gradually reduces, causing a decrease in the bed temperature, and
causing a change of slope for the reaction and liquid fractions. In addition, it can be
seen from the figure that the higher the hydrogen pressure, the greater the rise in

ep

4 Conclusion

In this paper, an innovative honeycomb fin metal hydride reactor integrated with

PCM is presented, based on LaNi5 hydrogen storage alloy as medium. A 3D CFD model
is established to investigate its performance. In addition, the effects of key factors are
also studied. The main conclusions are as follows:

pe
er
re
v

(1) The presence of the fins increases the rate of the hydrogen storage, thanks to
the enhanced thermal management of the reactor. Within the reaction time of 8000s,

during the absorption process the hydrogen storage capacity of the reactor without fins
and with hexagonal honeycomb fins is 1.232wt.% and 1.326wt.%, respectively, and
during the desorption process the reactor without fins and with hexagonal honeycomb
fins are 0.923wt.% and 1.326wt.%, respectively. The time taken to complete a cycle of
hydrogen absorption and desorption is reduced by 12250s. However, due to the
presence of the fins, the gravimetric and volumetric storage densities of the proposed
MH reactor are lower than those of the reactor without fins (0.411% and 14.76 kg of
H2 per m3, against 0.642% and 16.52 kg of H2 per m3, respectively).

(2) During the absorption and desorption processes, the mean saturated rate for the

ot

developed reactor is faster than that for the reactor without fins.
(3) In general, the smaller the fin size, the denser the distribution of fins in the
reactor, which slightly accelerates the reaction rate. The hydrogen absorption process

tn

increases by about 7% and the hydrogen desorption process increases by about 12%.
(4) With the increase of PCM thickness, the total mass and volume of the reactor
increase, resulting in a decrease in the gravimetric and volumetric storage densities, and

rin

an increase in the mean saturated rates.
(5) The higher the solid and liquid thermal conductivities are, the faster the

hydrogen absorption and desorption rates of the reactor, the time for the hydrogen

ep

absorption process to reach 90% reaction fraction is reduced by 20% and 71%,
respectively, and the hydrogen desorption process is reduced by 20% and 70%.
(6) The lower the initial temperature is, the higher the hydrogen pressure is, the

Pr

greater the reaction driving force is, the faster the hydrogen absorption rate is, while the
slower the hydrogen desorption rate is.
