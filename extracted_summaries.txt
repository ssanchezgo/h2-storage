Conclusion
This study presents a novel tank structure and honeycomb configu­
ration, offering an integrated hydrogen supply system that utilizes
exhaust heat from PEMFC to enhance desorption performance in MH
tanks. The key findings are summarized as follows:
1. The split-tank strategy shows that dividing a large MH tank into

addressing the thermal bottlenecks present in large tanks.

PEMFC’s cooling efficiency or power output. At a power level of 215
W, the system reaches thermal equilibrium and maintains a steady
hydrogen flow rate of 1000 sccm for over 30 min. The heat recovery
efficiency of 22.62 % demonstrates the viability of direct thermal
coupling between the PEMFC and MH.
3. Using multilayer cartridges increases the effective heat exchange
area and distributes the thermal load, allowing each layer to reach
higher steady-state temperatures. With two layers, the system sus­

CONCLUSION
Solid hydrogen carriers like metal hydrides offer advantages compared to state-of-the-art high
pressure or liquid hydrogen storage technology. Because hydrogen is chemically bound to a host
metal, the volumetric storage density can go up to 150 kg H2/m³ at low operation pressures in the

operation dynamics and system storage density. Modern metal hydride devices allow hydrogen
uptake and release cycles in the order of a few minutes only. The safe operation of metal hydride

ABSTRACT
Effective thermal management is a critical bottleneck for large-scale metal hydride (MH) hydrogen storage
systems, demanding innovative heat exchanger (HX) designs. This study numerically investigates a novel,
modular 400 kg-class Metal Hydride Reactor (MHR). The reactor features a hybrid dual-circuit HX that
combines an internal finned tube with an external helical coil. Our analysis is based on a multi-physics
model validated against experimental data. The analysis reveals a performance bottleneck that shifts from
external convection to internal conduction; increasing the heat transfer fluid (HTF) velocity reduces the peak
bed temperature by 15.7 K, but demonstrates clear diminishing returns. A comparative analysis confirms the
synergistic advantage of the dual-circuit design, which significantly outperforms configurations using only
internal or external cooling. Increasing the bed’s solid-phase thermal conductivity from 1.6 to 9.6 W/(m K) is
shown to shorten the 90% absorption time by approximately 24%. Furthermore, the hydrogen absorption rate is
found to be predominantly governed by the thermodynamic driving force; increasing the supply pressure from
5 to 9 MPa halves the 90% absorption time to 3600 s. These findings establish a quantitative framework for
co-optimizing system components, paving the way for efficient and scalable next-generation hydrogen storage
systems based on this modular reactor design.
